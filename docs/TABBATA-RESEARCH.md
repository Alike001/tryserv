# Tabbas — Problem and Competitor Research

**Research reviewed:** 14 November 2026  
**Purpose:** Ground the hackathon pivot in a real, specific problem and avoid presenting a generic AI document dashboard as novel.

## 1. Problem evidence: Nigeria

### 2020 Nigeria civil-society research

The GAPS / Women for Women International / WILPF Nigeria report, *Supporting Civil Society and Women’s Rights Organisations in Fragile and Conflict Affected Contexts — Nigeria Report*, says the Nigeria research included 25 key-informant interviews with organisations across five geopolitical zones. It records that reporting requirements varied by partner, donor and project; formats included narrative and financial templates sent by email and donor portals; reporting cadence also differed. Interviewees described some reporting processes as challenging and vague. The report recommended simplifying and harmonising financial and narrative reporting requirements.

Sources:
- [Nigeria report PDF](https://gaps-uk.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Supporting-Civil-Society-and-Women%E2%80%99s-Rights-Organisations-in-Fragile-and-Conflict-Affected-Contexts-Nigeria.pdf)
- [GAPS report page](https://www.qgaps.com/the-key-to-change-supporting-civil-society-and-womens-rights-organisations-in-fragile-and-conflict-affected-contexts-nigeria-report.html)

This is a relevant qualitative signal, not proof that every Nigerian NGO has the same workflow or that Tabbas has product-market fit.

### 2026 Nigeria civil-society landscape research

WACSI's April 2026 announcement describes a national study commissioned by WACSI Node Nigeria and led by the Nigeria Network of NGOs, based on 693 validated CSO responses, 21 key-informant interviews, a literature meta-scan and 47 capacity-provider case studies. It reports that 76.3% of surveyed organisations had not conducted a formal programme evaluation in the preceding two years; 100% reported no use of automated data analytics tools; 88.5% did not use donor-management or CRM systems. The announcement also reports constraints in fundraising, training, digitisation and data protection.

Sources:
- [WACSI press release](https://wacsi.org/press-release-nigerias-civil-society-sector-caught-in-a-systemic-starvation-cycle-as-new-national-study-reveals-deep-structural-and-capacity-crisis/)
- [WACSI summary](https://wacsi.org/eight-in-ten-nigerian-csos-have-no-written-fundraising-plans-new-study-by-wacsi-node-nigeria-reveals/)

These figures describe the sampled sector and should not be converted into claims that all organisations lack reporting systems. They support the need to validate a low-friction tool with smaller organisations.

### 2025 Nigeria civil-society country report

The EU SEE / HIVOS Nigeria Country Focus Report (June 2025) says donor requirements such as CAC registration and organisational policies can exclude some CSOs, and identifies limited capacity in proposal writing and financial management. It specifically says excessive reporting requirements and pressure to align with donor agendas reduce flexibility, with smaller organisations less able to negotiate terms.

Source:
- [EU SEE / HIVOS — Nigeria Country Focus Report, June 2025](https://eusee.hivos.org/assets/2025/12/Nigeria-NEW-CFR-TEMPLATE-v2.pdf)

This strengthens the problem context, but does not show that report-pack checking is the highest-priority pain or that organisations will pay for this product.

### Interpretation

The evidence supports a plausible pain: reporting requirements and formats can vary across funders, and smaller organisations may have limited data/administrative capacity. It does **not** establish demand for AI review specifically. The product hypothesis must be validated through interviews with at least one NGO programme/M&E officer and one finance/report reviewer.

## 2. Competitor landscape

### Outcome Proof — closest workflow competitor

[Official site](https://www.outcomeproof.com/) · [Features](https://www.outcomeproof.com/features/)

Outcome Proof captures evidence (quotes, numbers, photos and notes), links it to outcomes, manages grantmakers and deadlines, and drafts report sections with an AI assistant that shows the evidence used. It exports reports to Word and supports a $39/month flat-rate plan per its current public site.

**Implication:** Tabbas must not claim to invent evidence-linked reporting or AI report drafting. Its narrower proposed wedge is a one-off pre-submission consistency/evidence review of an already prepared narrative + financial schedule + attached evidence bundle, with deterministic financial checks and exact-quote validation.

### MIZAN Qiyas — adjacent evidence-review competitor

[Official site](https://mizan-qiyas.com/welcome/en.html)

MIZAN Qiyas targets government digital-transformation measurement. It checks uploaded evidence against requirements, identifies missing points, creates tasks and reports, and keeps humans in the decision loop.

**Implication:** Requirement-to-evidence review is not unique. Tabbas must differentiate by target user (small implementing NGOs), workflow (donor report pack before submission) and document mix (narrative, financial schedule and supporting attachments), not by claiming to be the first evidence-checking AI.

### DevResults — established M&E platform

[Official knowledge base](https://help.devresults.com/help/devresults-information-requirements)

DevResults supports structured monitoring and evaluation information including projects, organisations, results frameworks, indicators, locations, users and reporting periods.

**Implication:** Do not rebuild a full M&E platform, results framework manager or portfolio dashboard.

### ActivityInfo

[Grant-management system](https://www.activityinfo.org/about/grant-management-system.html)

ActivityInfo provides grant-management capabilities around applications, budgets, compliance and program data.

**Implication:** Avoid portfolio administration, grant lifecycle management and broad compliance-system scope.

### Eri — Nigeria field intelligence

[Official site](https://www.useeri.com/)

Eri describes itself as a field-intelligence company gathering evidence directly from people, places and markets across Nigeria through field teams and direct observation.

**Implication:** Tabbas cannot claim that documents prove physical delivery. Field verification is a different service and remains outside the product.

## 3. What is and is not differentiated

**Not differentiated by itself:**
- Uploading documents and summarising them.
- Mapping evidence to requirements.
- Drafting donor reports with AI.
- A dashboard showing red/amber/green statuses.
- Budget vs actuals.

**Potentially differentiated when implemented well:**
- A focused pre-submission QA pass over an already prepared donor report pack, rather than an ongoing evidence-capture and grant-management workspace.
- Cross-reading the narrative, financial schedule and supporting attachments to surface mismatched figures, unsupported claims and contradictory records.
- Deterministic row-level financial reconciliation (planned next; the current MVP only compares the two totals entered by the reviewer) combined with SERV reasoning for ambiguous semantic relationships.
- Exact quotations tied to page/sheet/row source locators, validated by code.
- A clear distinction between "not found in this bundle" and "false in the real world".
- A low-friction workflow designed for smaller Nigerian implementing organisations and varied donor templates.

**Important competitor correction:** Outcome Proof already offers ongoing evidence capture, outcome tracking, grantmaker deadlines, team collaboration, report generation and optional AI drafting from selected evidence. It also says its AI draft identifies the evidence used and reports when evidence is insufficient. Therefore Tabbas must not claim that evidence-linked AI reporting is new. Its proposed wedge is the one-off review of a pre-existing report bundle for cross-document inconsistencies and omissions, with deterministic financial reconciliation. This wedge still needs to be proven in user tests.

Sources:
- [Outcome Proof feature set](https://www.outcomeproof.com/features/)
- [Outcome Proof FAQ and AI/reporting details](https://www.outcomeproof.com/faq/)

This is a product hypothesis, not a defensible moat yet.

## 5. OpenServ track fit

OpenServ Edition 02's official page says every track must use SERV Reasoning. The Open Track accepts an idea in any domain and stack, provided it is built on SERV Reasoning. Submission requires a public repository, demo link or video, and a short write-up explaining the product, SERV usage and track. The page explicitly allows an existing project if the hackathon work is meaningful and clearly explained. It lists judging criteria as creativity, user readiness and revenue potential.

Sources:
- [Official Edition 02 page](https://www.openserv.ai/hackathon-2)
- [SERV Chat Completions API](https://docs.openserv.ai/serv-reasoning/api/chat-completions)

The Chat Completions reference documents `reasoning_effort`, function tools, `tool_choice`, token limits and standard completion metadata. Tabbas will use those documented API features. It will not claim unverified native OpenServ agent products.

## 6. Validation still required

Before calling the product-market fit proven:
1. Interview one Nigerian NGO programme/M&E officer about their last donor report pack.
2. Interview one finance/report reviewer about common reconciliation failures.
3. Ask to see a redacted or synthetic version of a real reporting checklist (never request beneficiary data).
4. Test whether users would pay per report or per month.
5. Compare the same fixed synthetic cases with and without SERV.

Do not claim these interviews or tests have happened until they actually do.
